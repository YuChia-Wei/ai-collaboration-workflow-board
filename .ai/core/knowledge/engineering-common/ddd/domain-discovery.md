# Discover domain meaning

Use this topic when terms, rules or responsibilities have competing meanings.
Start with the question that blocks the current work, rather than collecting
every possible domain concept.

## Establish a bounded question

State whose activity is being supported and what decision the model must explain.
Identify the people who can confirm business meaning.
Record the source and status of each important statement:
accepted requirement, expert statement, observed behavior, proposal or unknown.
A code path shows current behavior; it does not automatically establish policy.
An AI inference may suggest a question but cannot supply its acceptance.

For cancellation, ask what the requester expects to have stopped.
An order commitment, warehouse preparation, delivery movement and payment
obligation may have different owners and completion conditions.
Do not combine those meanings merely because people use the same verb.

## Explore concrete examples

Ask for one ordinary case and one case that changes the answer.
Use supplied identities, states and times; label invented teaching data.
For each example, ask what happened, what should follow and who can observe it.
Ask what must remain unchanged when the action is refused.
Separate facts available at the decision from facts learned later.
If the answer depends on an unknown policy, retain both the question and its owner.

A useful cancellation discussion might distinguish:
- an order for which delivery preparation has not started;
- an order already handed to a delivery organization;
- a payment operation whose result is not yet known.

These are investigation prompts, not accepted eligibility rules.
No cancellation deadline or refund-failure policy follows from these examples.

## Refine the language

Write short sentences using the domain experts' words.
Ask different participants to explain the same sentence with concrete data.
Record synonyms, conflicting meanings and the scope of each term.
Use a qualified phrase when a shared word conceals different responsibilities:
“order cancellation accepted” and “delivery cancellation confirmed”, for example.
Do not resolve disagreement by choosing the shortest class name.

Positive example:
“Order cancellation was accepted; delivery cancellation is awaiting a response.”
This exposes separate facts without claiming that either fact implies the other.

Counterexample:
“Everything is Cancelled because the Order status changed.”
This hides the meaning and authority of the external outcomes.

## Use evidence to advance the discussion

For each candidate rule, retain a supporting example and a challenging example.
Ask whether the rule is universal, conditional or specific to this case.
Identify which missing answer blocks design and which can remain outside scope.
Return a compact vocabulary, candidate rules, examples and owner questions.
Use the target's existing requirement or decision records when they already suffice.
The optional context-language template can capture unresolved terminology;
it does not turn a filled row into an accepted business definition.

## Stop conditions and limits

Stop the affected conclusion when authoritative sources conflict.
Do not approve a deadline, entitlement or financial outcome on an expert's behalf.
Do not invent an owner merely to complete a model.
Continue unaffected exploration while identifying the unresolved dependency.
Discovery may end with useful questions rather than a finished class model.
No workshop format, event catalogue or template is mandatory for every request.

## Review the result

Can an expert recognize the examples without translating technical jargon?
Can a reader distinguish agreement from a suggestion?
Is every consequential unknown connected to an owner or an explicit ownership gap?
Are different meanings preserved rather than silently normalized?

Return to [the topic index](README.md) when another modeling question emerges.

Ubiquitous language evolves within a bounded context through collaboration among
domain experts, the model and implementation. Do not require one uniform vocabulary
across the entire enterprise when contexts assign different meanings.
