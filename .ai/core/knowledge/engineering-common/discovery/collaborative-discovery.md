# Collaborative discovery with a stopping point

Use this method to resolve a bounded uncertainty through sources, examples and
short exchanges. Its purpose is a better next decision, not an exhaustive
questionnaire or a compulsory stage before every task.

## Establish the useful outcome

Name the question, affected actor and outcome at stake.
State what is already settled and which uncertainty changes the next action.
Reuse the caller's scope and explicit stopping conditions.
If these are missing, propose a small question batch and reassess its value
before expanding. A caller-selected timebox or budget takes precedence.

## Build an evidence view

Read available, permitted sources before asking for information they contain.
Keep four categories visible when material:
- facts/observations, with their inspected or attributed source;
- accepted decisions, with the authority and exact scope of acceptance;
- proposals, with reasons and the decision or evidence needed;
- unknowns/conflicts, with consequences and an owner or ownership gap.

Do not silently turn an observation into a business rule.
Do not treat an AI suggestion as an expert decision.
Do not re-ask an accepted choice unless a new contradiction requires attention.
Unavailability of a source is an evidence gap, not proof that its content is absent.

## Spend questions on decisions

Select a small batch of questions that can change the result.
One to three related questions often permit a useful exchange, but the task
and caller determine the appropriate batch size.
State why each answer matters and use a concrete case if it clarifies the choice.
Offer a recommended option only when sources or explicit constraints support it.
Explain its reason and material tradeoff; retain its proposed status.
Present real alternatives without concealing uncertainty or forcing agreement.

Positive example:
“Your accepted scope excludes external writes. I recommend a read-only preview
first because it permits review within that scope. Is a preview sufficient for
this milestone, or is a separately authorized write needed?”

Counterexample:
“Should we implement the entire workflow?” after the user already selected a
small preview. This reopens settled scope without new evidence.

## Separate decisions from empirical questions

A domain owner decides business meaning, entitlement and policy.
A provider capability, performance limit or operational failure may require
observation or an experiment instead of a preference question.
Identify the smallest evidence-gathering action and its authorization needs.
An unexecuted experiment remains proposed; a failed or inconclusive observation
remains failed or inconclusive. Do not replace missing evidence with consensus.
Keep the affected conclusion open while advancing independent parts.

## Check meaning through examples

Use an ordinary case and a meaningful differing case when they expose a rule.
Attribute invented data as illustrative rather than historical fact.
Ask what observers would see and what must remain unchanged on refusal.
Do not invent a threshold, deadline or financial policy to finish the example.
Select deeper BDD discovery or DDD domain-discovery guidance only when the
question calls for it and installed resource access is verified and allowed.
No scenario set, architecture model or fixed document format is mandatory.

## Reassess and stop

After a reply or new observation, update the evidence view.
Ask whether another question would change the next decision.
Stop when the agreed outcome is reachable, the timebox/budget ends, the caller
stops, or progress depends on an unavailable source or owner decision.
State remaining uncertainty and its practical impact rather than declaring
completion merely because the question list is exhausted.

Return a concise conclusion, source/status references, decisions, proposals and
open dependencies. Name the next action and owner or ownership gap.
Use existing records when persistence is requested; this method creates no
mandatory record family, approval ceremony or universal discovery pipeline.
It does not adopt target rules, approve requirements, choose architecture,
select technology, execute experiments or authorize implementation.
